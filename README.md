## 🧠 Chapter 1 — What is CS, really?

> *Most people waste their first year not knowing what direction they even want. Spend 10 minutes here. It'll save you months.*

### It's not just coding

Computer Science is the study of **how to solve problems using computers.** The code you write is just the tool — like how a carpenter uses a hammer. The real skill is knowing what to build, why, and how to think about building it.

CS is everywhere. The app you order food on, the algorithm behind your YouTube recommendations, the system protecting your bank account, the map calculating your route — all built by people who learned the exact same fundamentals you're about to learn.

### What fields exist inside CS?

| Field | What you actually build | Who hires |
|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| 🌐 Frontend Dev | Interfaces users see and interact with | Every tech company |
| ⚙️ Backend Dev | Servers, databases, APIs behind the scenes | Amazon, Zomato, Razorpay |
| 📊 Data Science / ML | AI models, predictions, analytics | OpenAI, Tesla, Flipkart |
| 🔐 Cybersecurity | Protecting systems from attacks | Banks, CrowdStrike, DRDO |
| ☁️ DevOps / Cloud | Making software fast, stable, scalable | AWS, Google, every startup |
| 📱 Mobile Dev | Android and iOS apps | Every startup and Big Tech |
| 🎮 Game Dev | Video games | Gaming studios, indie |

### Common myths — busted honestly

❌ **"I need a CS degree to get a software job"**
More than half of working developers are self-taught or from non-CS backgrounds. Companies care about what you can build and prove.

❌ **"I'm from ECE/EEE/Mechanical — this isn't for me"**
Your branch doesn't define you. It never did. See the [Non-CS section](#-non-cs-branches--tech-jobs).

❌ **"I need to be amazing at maths"**
Basic maths is enough for most software jobs. Only ML/AI and competitive programming need deeper maths.

❌ **"I need to buy courses to learn properly"**
This entire guide is proof that's false. Every resource here is free. The people who built Google, Meta, and Amazon learned from free resources too.

❌ **"I started too late"**
People switch to tech in their 30s and 40s successfully. You haven't started too late.

## 🌍 Chapter 2 — What's happening in tech right now?

> *Know the landscape before you pick your direction. Here's the honest picture — no sugarcoating.*

### The real situation in 2025/2026

AI is genuinely changing things. Not hype — real. But here's what it actually means for you:

- AI is **not** replacing all programmers
- AI **is** replacing programmers who stay average and don't adapt
- AI is creating massive demand for ML engineers, AI engineers, and data scientists
- Developers who use AI tools well are becoming 10x more productive and more hireable
- Companies now ask: not just "can you code?" but "can you build things, think through systems, and communicate clearly?"

The job market got tougher after 2022. Layoffs happened at big tech. But startups are still hiring aggressively, India's tech sector is growing fast, and remote work has opened up opportunities that didn't exist before. The bar has gone up — but being genuinely good? More valuable than ever.

### What companies actually want in 2026

1. **Real problem-solving** — can you think through something hard?
2. **Projects they can see** — what have you actually built?
3. **Communication** — can you explain technical things clearly?
4. **Fundamentals** — do you understand how things work, not just how to use them?
5. **Adaptability** — are you someone who keeps learning?

### Hottest fields right now

| Field | Demand | Why |
|-------|--------|-----|
| AI / ML Engineering | 🔥🔥🔥 | Every company is adding AI to their products |
| Full Stack Development | 🔥🔥🔥 | Always in demand, always will be |
| Cloud / DevOps | 🔥🔥🔥 | Everything is moving to cloud infrastructure |
| Cybersecurity | 🔥🔥🔥 | Cyberattacks are at an all-time high globally |
| Data Science | 🔥🔥 | Mature field, still growing steadily |
| Mobile Dev | 🔥🔥 | Billions of smartphone users, constant app demand |

## 🗺️ Chapter 3 — Pick your domain

> *Before you open a single resource — know what you're working toward.*

### Ask yourself honestly

- Does making things look beautiful and interactive excite you? → **Frontend**
- Do you like understanding how systems work behind the scenes? → **Backend**
- Are you fascinated by AI, data, and teaching machines to think? → **Data Science / ML**
- Do you enjoy finding flaws in systems and protecting them? → **Cybersecurity**
- Do you want to build apps people use on their phones? → **Mobile Dev**
- Do you want to make software reliable and scalable at huge scale? → **DevOps / Cloud**
- Do you just want to crack placements at a top company? → **General SWE / FAANG Prep**
- Are you from ECE/EEE/Mech and want to switch? → **Non-CS Path**

### Not sure yet?

That's completely fine. Start with **frontend or Python** — both are the easiest entry points, and after a month of either you'll have a much clearer feel for what you enjoy. Don't let indecision stop you from starting.

### Behavioral Interviews

> Don't underestimate this. Technical skills get you shortlisted. Behavioral answers get you hired.

**The STAR Method — use this for every single behavioral question:**

| | What to say | How long |
|-|------------|---------|
| **S** — Situation | Set the context. Where, when, what. | 10–15 seconds |
| **T** — Task | What was your specific responsibility? | 10–15 seconds |
| **A** — Action | What did YOU personally do? The most important part. | 30–45 seconds |
| **R** — Result | What happened? Numbers if possible. | 15–20 seconds |

**Free behavioral prep:**

| Resource | What it is |
|----------|-----------|

3. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your team
4. What is your biggest weakness?

## 📄 Chapter 6 — Resume & Portfolio

### Resume

**Honest tips nobody tells you:**

- **1 page. No exceptions for students.** If it's more, cut it.
- Every bullet point: **Action verb → What you did → The impact**
- "Built a REST API" is weak. "Built a REST API serving 10k requests/day with 99.9% uptime" is strong.
- **Use numbers wherever possible.** Numbers make vague things concrete and credible.
- Don't list skills you'd panic if asked about in an interview.
- **ATS** scans your resume before any human sees it. Simple formatting. No tables, no columns, no images in the main body.
- LaTeX resumes look more professional and are ATS-friendly. Use a template.

#### How to prepare — honest strategy

**Step 1** — Spend 1 week on concepts. Use IndiaBix or WCode to understand each topic properly before attempting timed practice.

**Step 2** — Practice 20–30 questions daily topic-wise. Track accuracy, not just completion.

**Step 3** — Once accuracy is good, focus on speed. Use WCode's 30-second tricks — speed is what makes or breaks aptitude rounds.

**Step 4** — Take 1 full company-specific mock test per week. PrepInsta has these for every major company. Simulate real exam conditions — timed, no breaks.

**Step 5** — In the last 2 weeks before a company's test, do their specific previous year papers. The pattern repeats more than you'd think.

> ⚠️ **Important:** Most companies have negative marking in aptitude rounds. 
> Don't guess randomly. Attempt what you're confident about, skip what you're not.

## ❓ FAQ — Honest Answers

**Which programming language should I start with?**
Python for AI/ML or quick results. JavaScript for web development. Java/C++ if college already teaches it well. Pick one and stick with it for at least 3 months. The language matters far less than the concepts you learn with it.

---

**How many LeetCode problems do I need to solve?**
150 well-understood problems across all patterns beats 500 problems you barely understood. Measure progress by pattern mastery, not problem count.

---

**Should I do competitive programming or just LeetCode?**
For getting a job this year → LeetCode patterns. For building long-term problem-solving strength → both. If placement is this year and time is limited, stay focused.

---

**I'm from ECE/EEE/Mechanical — can I really get a software job?**
Yes. Many software engineers at top companies came from non-CS backgrounds. See the [Non-CS section](#-non-cs-branches--tech-jobs).

---

**Is it too late to start in 3rd or 4th year?**
3rd year is ideal. 4th year is tight but doable if you're focused. Quality of effort beats amount of time.

---

**Do I need LeetCode Premium?**
No. Use [Leetracer](https://leetracer.com/screener) for free company-tagged questions.

---

**I keep losing motivation. How do I stay consistent?**
Motivation is temporary. Systems are permanent. Set a daily minimum so small that skipping it would feel embarrassing. Find an accountability partner in our Discord.

---

**I have no idea where to start. What do I do right now?**
Go to [cs50.harvard.edu](https://cs50.harvard.edu) and watch the Week 0 lecture. Come back here after. Don't overthink it.
